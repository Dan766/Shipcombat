Ship Combat — Interface

Camera & Controls

Look-directed combat: the direction you look picks the armed weapon. Broadsides stay fixed batteries — the rule the whole steering game rests on survives — but the camera goes free-look, the bow and stern get their own armaments, and holding aim converts the volley spread from a constant into something the player controls. This is a mockup of the scheme, not an implementation — see GAME_DESIGN.md for the write-up and game/README.md for what's actually playable today.

Look Sectors

camera yaw relative to heading → armed weapon

Four arcs tile the full 360° around the ship. A few degrees of hysteresis straddle each seam (shaded) so a camera resting on a boundary does not flicker between two armed weapons — see armedSectorFor() in scenes.js, the same function this diagram and the render verification both read.

The Four Sectors

same ship, same three opponents — only the camera moves

One player brig, three opponents placed ahead, abeam to starboard and astern. Port has no opponent in it on purpose — that panel is where the off-screen target indicator earns its place, rather than every panel conveniently having something to shoot.

Off-axis by design

The camera never orbits to exactly opposite the look direction. Square sails hang across the beam, so a dead-broadside camera turns them edge-on, and a dead-stern camera forehortens the hull nose-on — the same trap sceneClassSilhouette hit. Each panel's eye position is pulled ~35° back toward the ship's own bow/stern axis, keeping a three-quarter view of the hull in every sector. Measured, not eyeballed — see the render verification.

Aim & Spread

hold-to-aim is the mechanic worth the most

Tapping fire sends the volley at the standing spread — good against a close, jinking target. Holding aim (RMB) narrows BEARING_SPREAD / RANGE_SPREAD (game/src/sim/combat.ts) toward a tight pattern over about a second, at the cost of a steady bearing the whole time. Real aiming skill, without giving fixed-broadside guns any traverse.

Tap — wide pattern

Fires immediately at the standing spread. Forgiving of bearing error, right for a target that is close and moving fast across your arc.

Hold ~1s — tight pattern

Spread narrows the longer aim is held, toward roughly a third of the tap pattern — a placeholder curve, not a tuned one. Demands a steady bearing; rewards range and patience.

Specialty & Defence

held modifiers, available from any look direction

Mortars and swivel guns are not sectors — they are held-modifier modes layered over whatever the camera is currently armed with, matching the source material. Brace and the ram dash are the two defensive/aggressive commitments the scheme adds room for.

Not built

Ramming is depicted here, not implemented. game/README.md lists hull-to-hull collision as deliberately deferred — hulls currently pass straight through each other. A ram dash needs that collision to exist first; it is the single largest follow-on cost this scheme creates, named rather than buried.

Full Control Map

everything, including the parts still stretch goals
Open
  • Whether one mouse can carry both free-look and hold-to-aim without a second stick or button doing double duty — this reads fine on paper, less clear with a controller.
  • Whether brace is strictly dominant once the telegraph exists — if it is free to hold indefinitely with no speed cost worse than what it already does, there is no reason not to hold it on cooldown.
  • Whether the 60° bow and stern arcs are wide enough to select reliably mid-fight, or whether a fast yaw swing will overshoot past them under real mouse input.
